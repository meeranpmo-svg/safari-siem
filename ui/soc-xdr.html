XDR settings

Auto-case opens a case for an open incident of 2+ alerts at or above the score, at most 5 per minute and never twice. The case's playbook actions (block, disable, isolate) still wait for an approver - nothing is executed automatically. Changes are audited and apply within a minute. Only an administrator can change them.

How XDR works here

  1. Detection: the SIEM's detection rules raise alerts from every source it collects: endpoint (EDR, PowerShell, credential theft), identity (AD, Microsoft 365 sign-ins, privileged groups), network (FortiGate, Palo Alto, IPS, threat intel), email (Exchange, phishing, forwarding rules), web (WAF, proxy), data (DLP) and servers (Linux, Oracle, SQL).
  2. Correlation: alerts about the same user, host or internal IP within the link window become one incident. An entity seen in very many alerts, such as a domain controller or a scanner, is a hub and does not link, so one busy server cannot merge unrelated incidents. Hubs are listed under the table.
  3. Scoring: worst severity × 15, +10 per extra domain, +5 per extra ATT&CK tactic, +10 for a threat-intel match, +10 when UEBA rates a user or host high. Every point is listed on the incident.
  4. Response: Open case creates a case with the playbook's tasks, and its containment actions (block IP, disable account, isolate endpoint, revoke sessions) wait for an approver in Response & Approvals. With auto-case switched on under Settings, incidents at or above that score open a case automatically, up to 5 per minute and never twice. Approval is still required.
  5. Hunting: queries for activity below the alert thresholds, plus beaconing statistics. Leads lists UEBA behaviour findings that raised no alert, and chains of low-severity alerts that cross domains. A hit is a lead to look at, not proof.
